# Sample Assessment: Community Education Nonprofit

> Fictional composite created to demonstrate the diagnostic. It does not represent a named client or disclose confidential information.

## Situation

A growing virtual education nonprofit serves learners across multiple countries. Its small operations team wants to use AI to reduce manual learner-support work, identify disengaged students earlier, summarize facilitator reports, and help staff retrieve institutional knowledge.

The organization already uses an LMS, messaging platforms, spreadsheets, and several informal automations. Leadership is enthusiastic, but workflows and data ownership have developed unevenly during rapid growth.

## Diagnosis

The organization is not blocked by a shortage of AI ideas. Its primary constraint is process readiness. Automating undocumented work would likely encode inconsistency, produce fragile handoffs, and make accountability harder to trace.

Governance and measurement are the next constraints. A tool-first rollout could create privacy risk, uneven staff behavior, and no reliable way to determine whether the intervention works.

## Recommended first move

Run a four-week, human-in-the-loop workflow pilot for one bounded learner-support process.

### Weeks 1–2: Understand and design

- Select one high-volume, low-consequence support workflow.
- Map triggers, inputs, decisions, exceptions, handoffs, and current response time.
- Identify personal or sensitive data and establish permitted inputs.
- Assign a process owner and define where human review is mandatory.
- Record baseline volume, cycle time, rework, error rate, and learner satisfaction.

### Weeks 3–4: Test and learn

- Test AI assistance with a small internal group; do not automate final decisions.
- Review a sample of outputs for accuracy, tone, privacy, and harmful failure modes.
- Track adoption, time saved, corrections, escalations, and staff confidence.
- Hold a scale/pause/stop review against predetermined criteria.

## Decision gate

Scale only if the pilot demonstrates meaningful operational improvement without unacceptable quality, privacy, equity, or workload effects. Otherwise, redesign the workflow, strengthen the missing capability, or stop.

## Why this case belongs in the portfolio

The case demonstrates a central research and consulting proposition: AI adoption is not mainly a tool-selection problem. It is an organizational capability problem involving strategy, workflows, governance, people, infrastructure, and learning.
